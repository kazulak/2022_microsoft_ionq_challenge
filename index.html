How to Play QuanTris

The Core Concept

In Quantum Tetris, blocks represent qubits. Instead of clearing horizontal rows like in traditional Tetris, your goal is to clear blocks by creating destructive quantum interference between the falling active block and stationary blocks already on the board.

1. Qubit Shapes & Grid Alignment

A block is comprised of a Qubit Center (a glowing circle) and an Arrow indicating its state. The block physically occupies both the center cell and the target cell the arrow points to:

  • Arrow Right (cyan): state |0⟩. Covers the center and the cell to its right.
  • Arrow Up (violet): state |1⟩. Covers the center and the cell above it.
  • Arrow Diagonal (with a dot): superposition, e.g. |+⟩ = (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2 points up-right (blue). Covers the center and the diagonal cell.
  • A minus sign flips the arrow: −|0⟩ points left (red), −|1⟩ points down (green).

2. Applying Quantum Gates

Use keyboard keys or click the gate buttons to rotate/manipulate the falling block's state and physically change its shape:

  • X Gate (Key X): swaps |0⟩ ↔ |1⟩ — mirrors the arrow across the 45° diagonal.
  • Z Gate (Key Z): negates the |1⟩ part — mirrors the arrow across the horizontal axis.
  • H Gate (Key H): Hadamard, |0⟩ ↔ |+⟩ and |1⟩ ↔ |−⟩ — mirrors the arrow across the 22.5° line. Combining X, Z and H reaches all 8 directions.
  • CX (Key S) / CZ (Key A): two-qubit gates; they can entangle the qubits of a 2-qubit block.
  • Switch Target (Tab / Q): For 2-qubit blocks, toggle gate target between Left (Q1) and Right (Q2), marked by a pulsing white ring.

💥 How to Clear Blocks (Interference Examples)

To clear blocks, you must land your active block on top of a stationary block such that they occupy the exact same squares, and their state vectors are opposite (cancelling out), meaning vactive = -vboard (out of phase by 180°).

Example 1: Cancelling |0⟩ (Horizontal Qubits)

A stationary block is on the board in the -|0⟩ state (arrow pointing Left ←). To cancel it, make your falling block point Right → (+|0⟩) and land it on the same coordinates. They will cancel and both vanish!

Example 2: Phase Flipping for |1⟩ (Vertical Qubits)

A stationary block is on the board in the |1⟩ state (arrow pointing Up ↑). If your falling block is also in the |1⟩ state (↑), they will not cancel. First press Z to flip the falling block's phase to -|1⟩ (arrow pointing Down ↓). When they touch, they will cancel!

Example 3: Superposition & Rotations

Your falling block points Up-Right ↗. Pressing H turns it to point Right →, since H|+⟩ = |0⟩. By combining H, X and Z you can point your qubit in any of the 8 directions to cancel a target.

🔗 Entanglement & Mixed States (2-Qubit Blocks)

A 2-qubit block has two arrows. One-qubit gates act on the target qubit (switch with Tab / Q). The badge on the bridge shows the sign of the whole state: a + block and a − block with the same arrows cancel when they land in the same place (Z on a qubit in |1⟩ flips the sign).

  • Entanglement: CX or CZ on a superposition can entangle the two qubits, e.g. (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2. Entangled qubits have no direction of their own, so the arrows disappear, the block shrinks to its two centres, and the bridge glows.
  • Clearing: an entangled block cancels with an entangled block of the opposite sign in the same place. One-qubit and two-qubit blocks never cancel each other.

💡 Pro Tip: Ghost Block & Block Outlines

Look at the ghost block (the dashed outline below your block) to see exactly which cells your block will cover when it lands. Align the shapes first using left/right move keys, then apply quantum gates until the active block's arrows point in the opposite direction of the target block's arrows.
